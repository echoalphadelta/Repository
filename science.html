Supporting Science

Working Memory Limits

Cognitive psychologist Nelson Cowan reviewed decades of memory research and concluded that people can actively hold about four chunks of information in mind at once, which he calls "a single, central capacity limit averaging about four chunks" (Cowan, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 24, 87–185). When you're learning a system, or keeping track of where you are on the neck while you play, you need to keep all the shapes in mind together. Three shapes fit comfortably within that limit, but five (CAGED) is more than most people can hold at one time, especially as chord progressions happen.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922

Cognitive Load Theory

John Sweller's work shows that learners do better when unnecessary mental effort is removed (Sweller, Cognitive Science 12(2), 257–285, 1988). Decoupling shape names from pitch names removes a translation step. In CAGED, the player has to turn "E shape" into whatever chord it is at that fret. In Echo Alpha Delta, there is nothing to translate.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4

Interference

In CAGED, an "A shape" at the second fret is a B chord, so the shape name and the chord name compete. Echo, Alpha and Delta never clash with a chord name. It's the same kind of conflict as the Stroop effect, where reading the word "red" printed in blue slows you down. In J. R. Stroop's 1935 experiments, naming the ink colour of conflicting colour words took 74 percent longer than naming plain colour patches (Stroop, Journal of Experimental Psychology 18, 643–662). This is the core idea of the book.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0054651

Dual Coding

Allan Paivio's research found that information stored both visually and verbally is remembered better: "the additive effect of imagery and verbal codes is better than a verbal code alone" (Clark & Paivio, Educational Psychology Review 3(3), 149–210, 1991). Each shape has a name and a colour: Echo blue, Alpha red, Delta green. That gives two cues.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01320076
